# Chemistry reference

## Constants (CODATA / SI exact values where defined)
- Avogadro constant N_A = 6.02214076 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ (exact).
- Gas constant R = 8.314462618 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ = 0.082057 L atm mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ = 8.314 L kPa mol⁻¹ K⁻¹.
- Boltzmann constant k_B = 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹ (exact); Faraday constant F = 96485.33 C mol⁻¹.
- Planck constant h = 6.62607015 × 10⁻³⁴ J s (exact); speed of light c = 299792458 m s⁻¹ (exact).
- Molar volume of an ideal gas: 22.414 L mol⁻¹ at 0 °C and 1 atm (STP); 24.79 L mol⁻¹ at 25 °C and 1 bar.
- Water: Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25 °C; density ≈ 0.997 g mL⁻¹ at 25 °C; specific heat 4.184 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹.
- 0 °C = 273.15 K; 1 atm = 101.325 kPa = 760 mmHg; 1 bar = 100 kPa.

## Periodic trends
- Atomic radius decreases across a period (more nuclear charge) and increases down a group (more shells).
- Ionization energy and electronegativity increase across a period and decrease down a group. Fluorine is the most electronegative element (Pauling 3.98).
- Metals lose electrons (form cations); nonmetals gain electrons (form anions). Noble gases have full valence shells.
- Common charges: group 1 → +1, group 2 → +2, Al → +3, halogens → −1, O → −2, N → −3.
- Common polyatomic ions: NH₄⁺, OH⁻, NO₃⁻, NO₂⁻, SO₄²⁻, SO₃²⁻, PO₄³⁻, CO₃²⁻, HCO₃⁻, CH₃COO⁻ (acetate), MnO₄⁻, Cr₂O₇²⁻, CN⁻.

## Stoichiometry
1. Write and balance the equation (same number of each atom on both sides; balance O and H last for combustion).
2. Convert given quantities to moles: n = m / M, n = c × V, n = PV / RT.
3. Use the mole ratio from the coefficients; the limiting reagent is the one that runs out first (smallest n / coefficient).
4. Convert back to the requested unit. Percent yield = actual / theoretical × 100 %.
- Empirical formula: mass % → moles of each element → divide by the smallest → round to whole-number ratios.
- Example: CH₄ + 2 O₂ → CO₂ + 2 H₂O. 16.0 g CH₄ = 1.00 mol → 1.00 mol CO₂ = 44.0 g.

## Gases
- Ideal gas law PV = nRT (T in kelvin). Combined: P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂. Boyle (T const) P₁V₁ = P₂V₂; Charles (P const) V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂.
- Dalton: P_total = ΣP_i; partial pressure P_i = x_i P_total. Density ρ = PM / RT. Graham: rate₁/rate₂ = √(M₂/M₁).

## Solutions
- Molarity c = n / V (mol L⁻¹); dilution c₁V₁ = c₂V₂; molality b = n_solute / kg solvent.
- Mass percent = m_solute / m_solution × 100 %; ppm = mg L⁻¹ for dilute aqueous solutions.
- Colligative: ΔT_f = i K_f b (water K_f = 1.86 K kg mol⁻¹), ΔT_b = i K_b b (water K_b = 0.512), osmotic pressure Π = i c R T.

## Acids and bases
- pH = −log[H₃O⁺]; pOH = −log[OH⁻]; pH + pOH = 14.00 at 25 °C.
- Strong acids (fully dissociate): HCl, HBr, HI, HNO₃, H₂SO₄ (first proton), HClO₄. Strong bases: group 1 hydroxides, Ca(OH)₂, Sr(OH)₂, Ba(OH)₂.
- Weak acid: [H⁺] ≈ √(Ka × c) when dissociation < 5 %. pKa = −log Ka; Ka × Kb = Kw for a conjugate pair.
- Buffers (Henderson–Hasselbalch): pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]). Acetic acid pKa 4.76; NH₄⁺ pKa 9.25; H₂CO₃ pKa₁ 6.35.
- Titration: at the equivalence point n_acid × protons = n_base × hydroxides.

## Thermochemistry and equilibrium
- q = m c ΔT. ΔH_rxn = Σ ΔH_f(products) − Σ ΔH_f(reactants) (Hess's law). Exothermic ΔH < 0.
- Gibbs: ΔG = ΔH − TΔS; spontaneous when ΔG < 0; ΔG° = −RT ln K; ΔG° = −nFE°cell.
- Equilibrium constant K = [products]^coeff / [reactants]^coeff (pure solids and liquids omitted). Le Chatelier: a system shifts to oppose a change.
- Rates: rate = k[A]^m[B]^n; first order half-life t½ = ln 2 / k; Arrhenius k = A e^(−Ea/RT).

## Redox and electrochemistry
- OIL RIG: oxidation is loss of electrons, reduction is gain. Balance half-reactions in acid with H₂O and H⁺, then add OH⁻ for basic solutions.
- E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode. Nernst: E = E° − (0.05916 V / n) log Q at 25 °C. Charge Q = I t; moles of electrons = Q / F.

## Organic basics
- Functional groups: alcohol −OH, aldehyde −CHO, ketone C=O, carboxylic acid −COOH, ester −COO−, amine −NH₂, amide −CONH₂, ether −O−, alkene C=C, arene (benzene ring).
- Naming: longest chain (meth, eth, prop, but, pent, hex, hept, oct, non, dec) + suffix (-ane, -ene, -yne, -ol, -al, -one, -oic acid).
- Reaction types: substitution, addition (alkenes), elimination, condensation (esterification: acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water, acid catalyst), oxidation of alcohols (primary → aldehyde → acid; secondary → ketone).

## Lab safety
- Add acid to water, never water to acid (heat of dilution). Wear goggles and gloves; work in a fume hood with volatile or toxic substances.
- Never mix bleach (NaOCl) with ammonia (toxic chloramines) or with acids (chlorine gas). Hydrogen peroxide above 30 % is a strong oxidiser.
- Check the safety data sheet (SDS) for hazards, PPE and disposal; neutralise acid spills with sodium bicarbonate.
